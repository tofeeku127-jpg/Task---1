Question 1: Primary Characteristics & Hardware Generations

List four primary characteristics of a modern digital computer and identify the key hardware technology associated with each of the five generations of computers.

4 Primary Characteristics

⚡ Speed

Modern computers perform millions of complex mathematical calculations per second (measured in MIPS/FLOPS) without failure.

🎯 Accuracy

Computers produce high-precision outputs with near-zero errors unless fed invalid initial instructions (GIGO logic).

🏗️ Diligence

Unlike biological setups, machines run free from fatigue, concentration breaks, or workplace boredom over long intervals.

🔄 Versatility

Capable of processing diverse tasks back-to-back—transitioning from basic data formatting straight into deep graphic renders.

Five Generations of Hardware Technology

1st Generation (1940 - 1956)

Vacuum Tubes

Relied heavily on massive glass vacuum filaments. Consumed extreme energy, generated massive ambient heat, and required custom local spaces.

2nd Generation (1956 - 1963)

Transistors

Replaced glass tubes with solid-state crystalline semiconductor triggers. Shrinking machine dimensions while increasing operational frequency metrics.

3rd Generation (1964 - 1971)

Integrated Circuits (ICs)

Combined hundreds of separate active semiconductor components onto singular silicon structural matrices using photolithography steps.

4th Generation (1971 - Present)

Microprocessors (VLSI)

Very Large Scale Integration allowed packing millions of electronic switches onto a single microscopic microchip block, introducing consumer computing.

5th Generation (Present & Beyond)

ULSI & Artificial Intelligence

Ultra Large Scale Integration supports parallel processing structures alongside quantum material properties to handle complex modern neural learning arrays.

Question 4: Input Device Mechanisms & Sensitivity

Compare point-and-draw devices (such as a mouse and trackball) with graphics digitizers/drawing tablets, highlighting how their coordinate tracking mechanisms and pressure sensitivity differ for CAD and digital art applications.

🖱️ Point-and-Draw: Mouse & Trackball
Coordinate System: Relative Tracking

Measures displacement deltas from the last stationary vector state. Moving the pointer requires picking up and resetting the hardware physically across an external surface pad landscape.

Pressure Sensitivity: Binary (On/Off)

Standard microswitches only register on or off states. They cannot map the variable depth or thickness of a stroke based on how hard the user presses.

Best Choice for CAD

Excellent for panning infinite grids or editing exact numeric coordinate dimensions within drafting programs.

✍️ Graphics Digitizers & Drawing Tablets
Coordinate System: Absolute Mapping

The surface matrix maps 1:1 with screen boundaries. Placing the stylus on the top-right corner snaps the system pointer instantly to that exact pixel destination.

Pressure Sensitivity: Granular Force Tracking

Internal electromagnetic resonance hardware tracks up to 8,192 distinct micro-force thresholds to dynamically scale brush opacity or stroke width.

Best Choice for Digital Art

Provides the fluid control needed for organic illustration, mimicking real-world drawing mediums.

Question 5: Civil Engineering Blueprint Production Evaluation

Assess whether a drum/flatbed plotter or an ultra-high-definition monitor and large-format printer combination is superior for producing civil engineering blueprints, justifying your selection based on line resolution, scalability, and long-term production costs.

Strategic Verdict: The Ultra-HD Monitor + Large-Format Inkjet Printer Combination is highly superior for modern civil engineering offices, outperforming traditional vector plotters across multi-layered workflows.
🔍 Line Resolution Comparison

Traditional pen plotters trace raw vector paths with mechanical ink pens, achieving high continuous sharpness but struggling with complex raster details. Modern large-format inkjet arrays easily match this requirement using high-density configurations (up to 2400 x 1200 optimized DPI), cleanly printing precise, color-coded multi-layered engineering diagrams.

📐 Scalability and Workflow Flexibility

Plotters require physical pen changes and separate media rolls to adjust output scales. The digital monitor + large-format printer setup allows engineers to pan and verify massive infrastructure schematics instantly on an ultra-HD display, sending finalized drawings to an automated multi-roll printer that scales the output on demand.

💰 Production Costs & Efficiency

Pen plotters incur high operational costs due to mechanical pen drying, tearing risks, and slow print speeds. Inkjet systems lower production expenses by using cost-effective bulk ink tank reservoirs. They speed up production timelines and reduce paper waste by enabling digital proofing on screen before printing.
